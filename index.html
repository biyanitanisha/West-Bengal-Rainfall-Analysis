About the data

What is CHIRPS?

Climate Hazards Center InfraRed Precipitation with Station data (CHIRPS) is a quasi-global gridded rainfall dataset covering 1981 to near-present. It blends satellite-based infrared rainfall estimates with in-situ rain-gauge observations to give spatially continuous rainfall, useful where gauges are sparse or unevenly distributed. This study uses CHIRPS V3 Daily Reanalysis.

4Anomaly

2025 rainfall anomaly

The anomaly compares June–September 2025 with the 1991–2020 baseline. Positive values mean wetter than normal, negative values mean drier than normal.

Anomaly (mm) = 2025 Rainfall − 1991–2020 Climatology
Anomaly (%) = [(2025 Rainfall − Climatology) / Climatology] × 100
Reading the maps: blue tones show rainfall above the long-term baseline, warm tones show a deficit. Districts are outlined in grey.

Key takeaway

The normal pattern is wettest in the northern sub-Himalayan districts and moderate across the plains and southern Gangetic region. The 2025 anomaly maps and district chart show where this year departed from that pattern.
